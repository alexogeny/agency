# Creative-work assessment

Start with the work’s declared genre, audience, ambitions, and stage of draft.
Do not treat conventional realism, linear plotting, grammatical uniformity, or
commercial norms as universal requirements. Distinguish deliberate technique
from accidental inconsistency by its effect on the work, not speculation about
the author’s intention.

Select criteria suited to the form, such as:

- **Artistic intent and effect:** what experience the work creates and how
  consistently its choices serve that effect.
- **Structure and movement:** pacing, escalation, juxtaposition, scene or stanza
  function, and the handling of beginnings and endings.
- **Voice and language:** specificity, rhythm, imagery, dialogue, point of view,
  tonal control, and freshness without rewarding ornament for its own sake.
- **Character, setting, and world:** depth, coherence, agency, relationships,
  atmosphere, and selective detail where those elements apply.
- **Theme and resonance:** ideas embodied through the work rather than merely
  announced, with productive ambiguity where appropriate.
- **Genre/form execution:** fulfilment, transformation, or purposeful rejection
  of relevant conventions.
- **Technical control:** continuity, grammar, formatting, and mechanics insofar
  as they help or obstruct the intended reading experience.

Support judgements with precise moments and patterns across the work. Avoid
rewriting passages into the assessor’s preferred voice. Prioritise revisions
that unlock the work’s own aims, and label taste-dependent reactions as such.
