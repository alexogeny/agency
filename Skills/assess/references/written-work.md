# Written-work assessment

Choose criteria that fit the document’s purpose; do not force an academic essay
framework onto a business case, report, literature review, or technical guide.

## Requirements and evidence

- Check required sections, length rules, audience, formatting, citation style,
  source constraints, and submission components before judging prose quality.
- Apply stated word or page limits exactly, including any rule that excludes or
  disregards content beyond a cutoff. Record the counting method when length is
  consequential.
- Trace claims to sources where evidence matters. Audit citation/reference
  matching and source suitability, while distinguishing a malformed citation
  from a weak source or unsupported claim.
- Verify questionable references before alleging fabrication. If external
  verification is unavailable, describe what could and could not be checked.

## Select relevant quality criteria

- **Purpose and argument:** a clear, appropriately scoped aim or claim that
  responds to the task and develops logically.
- **Reasoning and evidence:** relevant support, warranted conclusions, critical
  judgement, and treatment of uncertainty or counterevidence.
- **Synthesis:** relationships among sources or ideas rather than a sequence of
  isolated summaries.
- **Organisation:** purposeful structure, coherent progression, informative
  sections, and effective transitions.
- **Audience and expression:** clarity, precision, concision, tone, terminology,
  grammar, and accessibility appropriate to the intended reader.
- **Presentation and attribution:** required style, usable tables/figures,
  accurate citations, and consistent references.

Do not double-penalise one defect across overlapping criteria unless the rubric
clearly makes the defect consequential in each. Explain any unavoidable overlap.
